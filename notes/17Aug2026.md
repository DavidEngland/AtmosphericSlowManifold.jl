**Why Polynomial Basis Functions Struggle with Time-Series Scale Separation**

Applying Gegenbauer, Chebyshev, or Legendre polynomials to a temporal time series $w(t)$ over a 30-minute record introduces severe limitations for wave-turbulence decomposition:

* **Global Support vs. Intermittent Physics:** Orthogonal polynomials have global support across the entire time interval. A single, localized 10-second gravity wave packet at $t = 12\text{ min}$ alters polynomial expansion coefficients globally across the entire domain, smearing time-localized events.
* **Loss of Dynamic Time-Scale Tracking:** The cospectral gap $s_{\text{gap}}(t)$ is inherently non-stationary. Continuous Morlet wavelets construct a 2D scale-time representation $(s, t)$ that evaluates local cross-spectral phase $\operatorname{Re}[W_w(s,t) W_\theta^*(s,t)]$, enabling instantaneous scale-gap tracking. Global polynomial coefficients cannot isolate time-varying scale boundaries without artificial sliding sub-windows.
* **Spherical Harmonics Misalignment:** Spherical harmonics ($Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$) are designed for 2D spherical spatial domains (global atmospheric GCMs), making them mathematically incompatible with 1D temporal sensor streams.
