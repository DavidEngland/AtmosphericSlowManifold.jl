Accounting for ground and substrate heat storage ($G$) is critical when assessing how site conditions modify the effective critical Richardson number ($Ri_c$). The Surface Energy Budget (SEB) at the land/ice surface balances net radiation against turbulent and conductive fluxes:

$$R_n = H + LE + G$$

In strongly stable nocturnal regimes (such as CASES-99) or polar sea-ice environments (such as SHEBA), net radiation $R_n$ is strongly negative. The subsurface flux $G = -k_s \left.\frac{\partial T_{\text{sub}}}{\partial z}\right\vert{}_{z=0}$ acts as the primary heat source moderating skin cooling. When $G$ or substrate thermal inertia is omitted, lower boundary buoyancy forcing is distorted, directly impacting near-surface temperature gradients $\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$ relative to wind shear $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}$ and altering the threshold where shear instability can maintain turbulence ($Ri_c$).

### Subsurface & SEB Data Availability Across Field Campaigns

Most major ABL field programs deployed dedicated soil or ice thermistors, though these measurements are frequently stored in auxiliary surface flux or soil files rather than standard atmospheric tower profile packages:

* **SHEBA (Arctic Sea Ice):**
* **Data Types:** Multi-level snow and ice thermistor strings (measuring profile temperatures from snow cover down through multi-year sea ice), snow mass balance probes, ice thickness, and ocean heat flux sensors.
* **SEB Role:** Snow thermal insulation dominates $G$. Deep-ice conductive flux buffers surface cooling during the polar night, keeping $Ri_g$ lower near the surface than atmospheric radiative cooling alone would predict.
* **Primary Archive:** UCAR/NCAR EOL SHEBA collection (`ice_phase` and `surface_flux` datasets).


* **CASES-99 (Kansas Grassland):**
* **Data Types:** Soil heat flux plates (typically at $5\text{ cm}$ depth) corrected with soil thermistors ($2, 5, 10, 20, 50\text{ cm}$ depth), soil volumetric water content probes, and infrared skin temperature radiometers at main and auxiliary flux stations.
* **SEB Role:** Dry grassland soil exhibits high nocturnal ground heat storage flux $G$, which supplies up to $50\text{ W m}^{-2}$ upward to offset net longwave loss, directly controlling the strength of surface temperature inversions.
* **Primary Archive:** NCAR EOL CASES-99 main tower and ABFlux auxiliary station archives.


* **BLLAST (Southern France Transition):**
* **Data Types:** Multi-site soil temperature and flux profiles across heterogeneous land-use patches (moorland, forest, corn, wheat) with skin temperature radiometers and soil moisture sensors.
* **SEB Role:** Substrate heat storage accumulated during daytime convective heating releases during the late afternoon transition (PAT), altering the decay rate of surface heat flux $H$ and modulating the collapse of $Ri_g$.
* **Primary Archive:** SEDOO / BLLAST campaign database (`SOIL` and `FLUX` data sub-directories).


* **FLOSS (Middle Park, Colorado):**
* **Data Types:** Soil/snow thermistors, snowpack depth sensors, and net radiometers in complex mountain valley terrain.
* **SEB Role:** Variable snow cover thickness and sloping terrain cause localized ground heat fluxes that generate katabatic drainage flows and localized shear layers.
* **Primary Archive:** NCAR EOL FLOSS-1 surface meteorology and soil package.

### Physical Mechanism: How Subsurface Coupling Influences $Ri_c$

$$\text{Subsurface Heat Flux } G \longrightarrow \text{Surface Buoyancy Flux } w'\theta'_0 \longrightarrow \text{Gradient } \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} \longrightarrow \text{Effective } Ri_c$$

1. **Thermal Coupling & Boundary Dynamics:** A site with high substrate heat supply ($G$) maintains a higher surface skin temperature $T_s$. This reduces near-surface static stability $\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$ for a given atmospheric cooling rate.
2. **Shear vs. Stratification Balance:** Because $Ri_g = \frac{g}{\theta_0} \frac{\partial \theta / \partial z}{(\partial u / \partial z)^2}$, a lower surface temperature gradient allows mechanical shear to maintain turbulent exchange at larger atmospheric stability scales, effectively raising or reshaping the observed empirical $Ri_c$.
3. **Implications for PDE Closures:** In 1D ABL formulations (like `AtmosphericSlowManifold.jl`), replacing fixed Dirichlet or Neumann skin boundary conditions with a conductive ground/ice layer flux balance allows site-specific surface parameters (soil diffusivity, snow depth, moisture) to enter the Galerkin momentum and heat operators naturally.
