Accounting for ground and substrate heat storage ($G$) is critical when assessing how site conditions modify the effective critical Richardson number ($Ri_c$). The Surface Energy Budget (SEB) at the land/ice surface balances net radiation against turbulent and conductive fluxes:

$$R_n = H + LE + G$$

In strongly stable nocturnal regimes (such as CASES-99) or polar sea-ice environments (such as SHEBA), net radiation $R_n$ is strongly negative. The subsurface flux $G = -k_s \left.\frac{\partial T_{\text{sub}}}{\partial z}\right\vert{}_{z=0}$ acts as the primary heat source moderating skin cooling. When $G$ or substrate thermal inertia is omitted, lower boundary buoyancy forcing is distorted, directly impacting near-surface temperature gradients $\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$ relative to wind shear $\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}$ and altering the threshold where shear instability can maintain turbulence ($Ri_c$).

### Physical Mechanism: How Subsurface Coupling Influences $Ri_c$

$$\text{Subsurface Heat Flux } G \longrightarrow \text{Surface Buoyancy Flux } w'\theta'_0 \longrightarrow \text{Gradient } \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} \longrightarrow \text{Effective } Ri_c$$

1. **Thermal Coupling & Boundary Dynamics:** A site with high substrate heat supply ($G$) maintains a higher surface skin temperature $T_s$. This reduces near-surface static stability $\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$ for a given atmospheric cooling rate.
2. **Shear vs. Stratification Balance:** Because $Ri_g = \frac{g}{\theta_0} \frac{\partial \theta / \partial z}{(\partial u / \partial z)^2}$, a lower surface temperature gradient allows mechanical shear to maintain turbulent exchange at larger atmospheric stability scales, effectively raising or reshaping the observed empirical $Ri_c$.

### Diagnostic Test Analysis (`test/test_H_scaling.jl`)

1. **TEST 1 (Steady-State Neumann Diffusion $K \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b}$):**
* **Result:** Passes independently of $H$.
* **Why:** In steady state, $\dot{\mathbf{a}} = \mathbf{0}$, so $M$ drops out. Because `_stiffness_matrix` correctly scales as $K = \frac{2}{H} K_x$ and the boundary vector $\mathbf{b} = F_0 \boldsymbol{\phi}(x_1)$ is unscaled, $K \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b} \implies \mathbf{a} \propto H$. Reconstructing $\frac{d\theta}{dz} = \frac{2}{H} \frac{d\theta}{dx}$ cancels $H$ exactly, recovering $\frac{d\theta}{dz} = -\frac{F_0}{K_m}$. Thus, $K$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are internally consistent for steady-state problems.


2. **TEST 2 (Generalized Eigenvalues of $(K, M)$):**
* **Result:** Fails with a $10\times$ discrepancy in the ratio between $H = 100\text{ m}$ and $H = 1000\text{ m}$.
* **Why:** The decay rates of the system are the eigenvalues of $M^{-1} K$.
* **Physical Scaling:**

$$M_{\text{phys}} = \frac{H}{2} M_x, \quad K_{\text{phys}} = \frac{2}{H} K_x \implies M_{\text{phys}}^{-1} K_{\text{phys}} = \frac{4}{H^2} M_x^{-1} K_x \propto H^{-2}$$


* **Unscaled Code Behavior:**

$$M_{\text{code}} = M_x \implies M_{\text{code}}^{-1} K_{\text{phys}} = \frac{2}{H} M_x^{-1} K_x \propto H^{-1}$$




* Because $M_{\text{code}}^{-1} K_{\text{phys}}$ scales as $H^{-1}$ instead of $H^{-2}$, comparing $H = 100\text{ m}$ to $H = 1000\text{ m}$ causes the ratio $\lambda_{\text{numeric}} / \lambda_{\text{analytic}}$ to grow by a factor of 10.

### Mathematical Proof of Metric Mismatch

Mapping physical domain $z \in [0, H]$ to reference domain $x \in [-1, 1]$ gives:

$$z = \frac{H}{2}(x + 1), \quad dz = \frac{H}{2} dx, \quad \frac{d}{dz} = \frac{2}{H} \frac{d}{dx}$$

#### 1. Weak Form PDE

$$\int_0^H \phi_i(z) \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial t} \, dz = -\int_0^H K_m \frac{d\phi_i}{dz} \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} \, dz + \left[ \phi_i(z) K_m \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} \right]_0^H$$

#### 2. Term-by-Term Coordinate Transformation

* **Mass Matrix Element ($M_{ij}$):**

$$M_{ij} = \int_0^H \phi_i(z) \phi_j(z) \, dz = \frac{H}{2} \int_{-1}^1 \phi_i(x) \phi_j(x) \, dx = \frac{H}{2} (M_x)_{ij}$$


* **Stiffness Matrix Element ($K_{ij}$):**

$$K_{ij} = \int_0^H K_m \frac{d\phi_i}{dz} \frac{d\phi_j}{dz} \, dz = \int_{-1}^1 K_m \left(\frac{2}{H} \phi_i'(x)\right) \left(\frac{2}{H} \phi_j'(x)\right) \left(\frac{H}{2} dx\right) = \frac{2}{H} \int_{-1}^1 K_m \phi_i'(x) \phi_j'(x) \, dx$$

#### Mathematical Origin of the 7.8% Gap

In a weighted Hilbert space $L_w^2([-1,1])$ with weight function $w(x) = (1-x^2)^{\lambda - 1/2}$:

$$\langle u, v \rangle_w = \int_{-1}^1 u(x) v(x) w(x) \, dx$$

Integrating the physical diffusion operator $- \frac{d}{dx} \left( K_m \frac{d\theta}{dx} \right)$ by parts against a test function $\phi_i(x)$ under this weighted inner product yields:

$$\int_{-1}^1 -\frac{d}{dx}\left(K_m \frac{d\theta}{dx}\right) \phi_i(x) w(x) \, dx = \int_{-1}^1 K_m \frac{d\theta}{dx} \frac{d}{dx} \left( \phi_i(x) w(x) \right) dx - \left[ K_m \frac{d\theta}{dx} \phi_i(x) w(x) \right]_{-1}^1$$

Applying the product rule to the test term gives:

$$\frac{d}{dx} \left( \phi_i(x) w(x) \right) = \phi_i'(x) w(x) + \phi_i(x) w'(x)$$

The current `_stiffness_matrix` computes:

$$K_{ij} = \int_{-1}^1 K_m \phi_j'(x) \phi_i'(x) w(x) \, dx$$

It omits the weight-derivative cross-term:

$$K_{ij}^{\text{weight}} = \int_{-1}^1 K_m \phi_j'(x) \phi_i(x) w'(x) \, dx$$

Where $w'(x) = -2x \left(\lambda - \frac{1}{2}\right) (1-x^2)^{\lambda - 3/2}$.

* When $\lambda = 0.5$, $w(x) = 1 \implies w'(x) = 0$, so the cross-term vanishes identically and the ratio is $1.0011$.

Reinterpreting the critical Richardson number $Ri_{\text{cr}} \approx 0.25$ from a static algebraic boundary into a fold singularity in state space provides a rigorous dynamical mechanism for the lingering, non-equilibrium turbulence observed during evening atmospheric transitions.

### 1. Geometric Singular Perturbation Theory (GSPT) Formulation

Consider the planetary boundary layer momentum dynamics expressed as a fast-slow system:


$$\varepsilon \dot{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}; \mathbf{p}), \qquad \dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}; \mathbf{p}), \qquad 0 < \varepsilon \ll 1$$

where $\mathbf{y}$ represents fast ageostrophic or high-order Galerkin modal amplitudes, $\mathbf{x}$ represents slowly evolving balanced shear and thermal profiles, and $\varepsilon$ parameterizes the scale separation.

* **Critical Manifold $\mathcal{S}_0$:** Defined by the zero-set of the fast vector field, $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \in \mathbb{R}^{m+n} : \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \mathbf{0}\}$.
* **Normal Hyperbolicity Loss:** The manifold $\mathcal{S}_0$ is normally hyperbolic where all eigenvalues of the fast Jacobian $\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}$ have non-zero real parts. The **fold locus** $\mathcal{L}$ occurs precisely where normal hyperbolicity breaks down:

$$\mathcal{L} = \left\{ (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \in \mathcal{S}_0 : \det(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}) = 0 \right\}$$



In physical space, $\mathcal{L}$ corresponds to the spatial profile condition where local shear can no longer overcome thermal stratification, matching $Ri_g(z,t) \to Ri_{\text{cr}}$.

---

### 2. The Canard Funnel & Transient Turbulence

In standard standard fold interactions, trajectories reaching $\mathcal{L}$ undergo a fast-jump transition away from the slow manifold (i.e., immediate collapse into laminar flow). However, when the fold line contains a **folded node** singularity, the local phase-space geometry opens a **canard funnel**.

```
                       Attracting Sheet S₀⁻ (Active TKE)
                                 \       /
                                  \     /  Canard Trajectories ("Whispers")
                                   \   /   linger for O(1) slow time
====================================\=/===================================== Fold Locus L
                                     X  <-- Folded Node (Pseudo-Singular Point)
====================================/=\===================================== det(D_y g) = 0
                                   /   \
                                  /     \
                                 /       \
                        Repelling Sheet S₀⁺ (Non-equilibrium)

```

1. **Crossing the Fold:** The folded node acts as a non-isolated pseudo-singular point where the fast and slow directions align.
2. **Canard Persistence:** Trajectories inside the funnel pass continuously from the stable, attracting sheet $\mathcal{S}_0^-$ across $\mathcal{L}$ onto the unstable, repelling sheet $\mathcal{S}_0^+$.
3. **Physical Interpretation ("Whispers of Turbulence"):** Rather than collapsing instantaneously to zero TKE when $Ri_g > Ri_{\text{cr}}$, the flow state remains bounded within an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ neighborhood of the repelling slow manifold for $\mathcal{O}(1)$ slow time scales. This lingering state sustains transient Reynolds stresses, explaining why field campaigns like **CASES-99** and **BLLAST** exhibit persistent intermittent turbulence during strong nocturnal surface cooling.

### 3. Adjugate Desingularization Mechanics

Implicit differentiation of $\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \mathbf{0}$ yields the slow-flow ODE on $\mathcal{S}_0$:


$$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}), \qquad \mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g} \, \dot{\mathbf{y}} = -\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{g} \, \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$$

Inverting $\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}$ causes a division by $\det(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g})$, generating a numerical singularity at $\mathcal{L}$ where stiffness triggers solver integration failure. `ASM.jl` resolves this by applying **Cramer's rule/adjugate transformation**:

$$\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}^{-1} = \frac{\operatorname{adj}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g})}{\det(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g})}$$

By introducing the rescaled fictitious time parameter $\tau$ defined via $d\tau = \frac{dt}{-\det(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g})}$, the singular system transforms into the smooth **desingularized vector field**:


$$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{d\tau} = -\det(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}) \, \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$$

$$\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{d\tau} = \operatorname{adj}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{g}) \, \mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{g} \, \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$$

This time-rescaling smooths out the numerical cliff, reversing direction on the repelling sheet so stiff integrators like `Rodas5P` can trace canard orbits across $\mathcal{L}$ without step-size collapse.
